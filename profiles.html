Quick Guide

Discover the different learning profiles !!

Understand the different methodologies of processing information. This section breaks down specific academic strengths, targeted study habits, and risk factors associated with each learning style, reminding you that these profiles are flexible tools meant to expand your academic toolkit, not define your limits.

The Active Learner

Active Learning Approach

You learn best by interacting with content. You get bored with passive reading and need to debate, explain out loud, teach concepts to others, or test yourself immediately to retain them.

Strengths:

Great retention capacity through verbal practice, excellent for teamwork and fast critical thinking.

Key Strategies:
  • Feynman Technique: explaining the topic as if teaching it to a beginner.
  • Study groups and debates on exam topics.
  • Use of flashcards and constant self-assessments.
Common Risk: Jumping into practice without establishing necessary theoretical foundations.

The Visual Organizer

Visual Learning Approach

Your mind works through images, diagrams, colors, and spatial connections. You prefer to see the global structure of a topic before diving into specific details.

Strengths:

Excellent capacity to synthesize complex information, identify patterns, and remember data locations in your notes.

Key Strategies:
  • Creation of mind maps, concept maps, and flowcharts.
  • Strategic use of color to categorize information hierarchies.
  • Converting long texts into infographics or logical schemes.
Common Risk: Spending too much time designing the perfect diagram instead of studying the content.

The Practice-Oriented Student

Practical Learning Approach

You learn by doing. You need to see real-world applications, solve practical exercises, face simulations, or build projects for theory to make sense.

Strengths:

Great problem-solving skills, quick adaptation to real scenarios, and excellent handling of applied logic.

Key Strategies:
  • Solving past practice exams from the first day of study.
  • Focusing on labs, case studies, and direct application exercises.
  • Relating abstract concepts to real-life situations or personal projects.
Common Risk: Getting frustrated or ignoring fundamental theory when practical exercises become too complex.

The Structured Planner

Structured Learning Approach

Your greatest ally is order. You feel in control when you break material into small goals, schedule reviews in advance, and follow a systematic step-by-step method.

Strengths:

Constant discipline, excellent time management, zero room for last-minute surprises, and low stress levels regarding deadlines.

Key Strategies:
  • Creating study calendars broken down by weeks and days.
  • Using checklists and time-blocking methods (like Pomodoro).
  • Establishing fixed study routines in an organized space.
Common Risk: Becoming so rigid with the plan that it's hard to adapt when unexpected events happen.
